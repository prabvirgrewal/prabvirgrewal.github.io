VEX Spin Up Competition Robot Competed

Team captain · Head builder · Programmer

4design iterations in one season

Spin Up asked robots to shoot foam discs into goals, turn field rollers, and expand at the end of the match. I led a team of four through the full design cycle, from first CAD to the Ontario Provincial Championship and then the VEX World Championship in Dallas, the largest robotics competition in the world, where we placed 11th in our division.

SubsystemFirst versionWhat we changed and why
Drive4-motor6-motor drive (4 omni + 2 traction wheels) for more pushing power and speed
ShooterCurved, 1 disc at a timeStraight flywheel shooter with a motorized indexer that fires 3 discs back to back
Flywheel controlFixed voltage, RPM dropped after each shotClosed-loop speed control using motor encoders so RPM recovers between shots
ExpansionMotorized, not enough torquePneumatic expansion that covers more tiles
RollersFast but weakRaised the sprocket ratio for more torque

We documented every meeting in a 141-page engineering notebook using the SPICE design process. Off the field, we ran a Girl Powered STEM event with a local library to get more students into robotics.

Line-Following Robot with Obstacle Avoidance Built

Computer Engineering · Solo project · 2025
Arduino Uno · C++ · 4WD

A 4-wheel-drive robot that follows black tape on the floor and drives around obstacles on its path, then finds the line again and finishes its route.

  • Two IR sensors under the front bumper steer the robot back over the tape
  • An HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor on a servo spots obstacles, looks left and right, and picks the side with more room
  • An L293D motor shield drives 4 DC gear motors with PWM speed control
  • Calibrated the IR sensors for consistent tracking and split the code into reusable functions for driving, sensing and avoiding
